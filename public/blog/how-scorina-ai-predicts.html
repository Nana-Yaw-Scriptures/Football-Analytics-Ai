Under the hood

How Scorina AI predicts football matches

Scorina AI turns raw football data into a clear prediction for every match — a win probability for each side, an expected-goals estimate and the most likely scoreline. Here's what happens between the data and the number you see.

Scorina AI · Methodology · Updated 19 August 2026

Every prediction on Scorina starts with the same question a good analyst asks: how many goals is each team likely to create and concede in this specific match? We answer it with data rather than opinion — recent results, scoring and conceding rates, expected goals, the venue, and the history between the two sides. Those signals are combined by a machine-learning model trained on thousands of past matches, which returns the probabilities you see on the app.

None of it is hand-typed. When a fixture appears, the engine gathers the numbers, builds the features, runs the model, and produces the prediction automatically. Below is that journey, step by step.

01

The prediction pipeline

Collect data

Form, goals, expected goals, standings and head-to-head for both teams.

Attack & defence strength, venue and form-weighted expected goals.

Run the model

Prediction

Win chances, expected goals and the likeliest scoreline.

02

The data behind every match

/ four signals

A prediction is only as good as what goes into it. For each fixture, Scorina pulls together four kinds of information about both sides.

Results & form

Recent matches for each team — wins, draws and losses, and the goals they scored and conceded.

Expected goals

How many goals each side should score and concede from the quality of chances, not just the score.

League standings

Season-long attack and defence strength — a stable baseline for how good each team really is.

Head-to-head

Recent history between the two teams — some matchups favour one side regardless of form.

03

Expected goals: the core

/ how xG is blended

The heart of a Scorina prediction is a projected xG for each team — our estimate of the goals they'll create. We blend several views of a team's attack and defence, each weighted by how telling it is: season form is steady but slow, recent form and the venue capture what's happening now, and head-to-head nudges it for the opponent.

Two corrections keep it honest: a strength multiplier for the gap in quality between the sides, and an xG regression step that pulls over- or under-performing teams back toward what they actually deserve.

04

From goals to a prediction

/ probabilities & scoreline

With a projected xG for each team, a machine-learning model trained on thousands of matches turns those features into three numbers that add to 100% — home win, draw, or away win — and estimates the likeliest scoreline. The relationship between expected goals and results isn't a straight line, which is where the model earns its keep.

06

Honest about the limits

/ what a model can't see

A model is a guide, not a crystal ball. Scorina predicts from the data it has — it doesn't know about a late injury, a rested squad before a cup tie, or the weather. Football is famously unpredictable, and upsets are part of the game. Our numbers tell you what's likely, framed as probabilities rather than certainties, and they're built for analysis and entertainment — not betting advice.
